The question
Write the line tangent to  x2 − 3xy + 4y = 8
through the point  (2, −2).
A tangent line needs two things: a point and a slope. We have the point — the slope is the derivative.
Step 1 — Check the point is on the curve
(2)2 − 3(2)(−2) + 4(−2) = 4 + 12 − 8 = 8 ✓
Left side = 8 = right side — the point is on the curve.
Always confirm this first — a tangent line only makes sense at a point on the curve.
Step 2 — Differentiate implicitly
x2 → 2x
−3xy → product rule!  −3y − 3xy′
4y → 4y′
8 → 0
2x − 3y − 3xy′ + 4y′ = 0
−3xy needs the product rule: −3[(x)′y + x·y′] = −3y − 3xy′.
Step 3 — Solve for y′
2x − 3y − 3xy′ + 4y′ = 0
gather the y′ terms, move the rest right:
y′(4 − 3x) = 3y − 2x
y′ = 3y − 2x4 − 3x
Step 4 — Slope at (2, −2)
y′ = 3(−2) − 2(2)4 − 3(2) = −6 − 44 − 6 = −10−2 = 5
the slope of the tangent line is m = 5.
Plug the point straight into the derivative — no need to solve for y first.
Step 5 — Point-slope form
y − (−2) = 5(x − 2)  ⇒   y + 2 = 5x − 10
y = 5x − 12
The green line just grazes the blue curve at (2, −2) — that's the tangent.
