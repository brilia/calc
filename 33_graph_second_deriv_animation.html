The question
Let  dydx = f(x) g′(x).  Find  d2ydx2 at x = 2.
Step 1 — It's a product, so use the product rule
dydx = f(x) · g′(x)
d2ydx2 = f′(x)g′(x) + f(x)g″(x)
y′ = fg′ is a product, so y″ = f′g′ + fg″. Four values to find at x = 2.
Step 2 — Read f(2) and f′(2) from the f graph
f(2) = 4    f′(2) = slope = 6 − 23 − 1 = 2
f is mid-rise at x = 2 — its peak is at x = 3.
Step 3 — Read g′(2) and g″(2) from the g′ graph
g′(2) = −1    g″(2) = slope = −3 − 04 − 1 = −1
g′ is mid-descent at x = 2 — its flat part is over x = 4 to 7.
Step 4 — Substitute into the product rule
d2ydx2 = f′(2)g′(2) + f(2)g″(2)
= (2)(−1) + (4)(−1) = −2 − 4 = −6
f′g′ = (2)(−1) = −2;  fg″ = (4)(−1) = −4;  sum = −6.
The answer
d2ydx2 at x = 2 is −6
