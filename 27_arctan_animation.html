The question
y = tan−1(4x)  — find the instantaneous rate of change at x = 12.
(A) 12 (B) 45 (C) 2 (D) −4tan2(2)
"Instantaneous rate of change" is just a verbal disguise for "derivative".
The tool — derivative of arctangent
ddu tan−1(u) = u′1 + u2
the u′ on top is the chain rule, built right in.
Memorize this one — it shows up constantly, and the u′ is easy to forget.
Step 1 — Name the parts
u  u = 4x   ⇒   u′  u′ = 4
Inside the arctangent sits 4x — that's u, and its derivative is 4.
Step 2 — Apply the formula
dydx = 41 + (4x)2
= 41 + 16x2
Square the whole 4x — (4x)² = 16x², not 4x².
Step 3 — Evaluate at x = 1/2
41 + 16(12)2 = 41 + 4 = 45
16 · 14 = 4,  so the denominator is 1 + 4 = 5.
Plug in carefully — the arithmetic is where these questions are usually lost.
The answer
(B)  45
(A) 12 (B) 45 (C) 2 (D) −4tan2(2)
(D) is the trap — tan⁻¹ means arctangent, not 1/tan. Its derivative is never −1/tan².
