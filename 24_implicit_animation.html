The question
Let  3x2 − 2y + 4xy = 6.  Find  dydx.
The equation mixes x and y — and y is secretly a function of x.
The key idea
Differentiate both sides with respect to x.
ddx[y] = y′   chain rule: y depends on x
every y you differentiate drags a y′ along with it.
That's the whole trick — and it's why y′ will show up more than once.
Step 1 — Differentiate term by term
3x2 → 6x
−2y → −2y′
4xy → product rule!  4y + 4xy′
6 → 0
6x − 2y′ + 4y + 4xy′ = 0
4xy needs the product rule: (4x)′·y + 4x·y′ = 4y + 4xy′.
Step 2 — Collect the y′ terms
6x − 2y′ + 4y + 4xy′ = 0
y′ appears twice — gather them on one side:
y′(4x − 2) = −6x − 4y
Move the non-y′ terms right, then factor y′ out of what's left.
Step 3 — Solve, then tidy up
y′ = −6x − 4y4x − 2  =  6x + 4y2 − 4x
divide top and bottom by 2:
y′ = 3x + 2y1 − 2x
Flip both signs, then divide by 2 — the tidy form.
The answer
dydx = 3x + 2y1 − 2x
differentiate → collect y′ → factor → divide
1 / 6
