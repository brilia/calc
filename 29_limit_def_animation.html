The question
limh → 0 sin−1(45 + h) − sin−1(45)h
(A) 0 (B) 2541 (C) 53 (D) does not exist
Before reaching for L'Hôpital — look at the shape of the expression.
Step 1 — Recognize the pattern
the limit definition of a derivative:
f′(a) = limh → 0 f(a + h) − f(a)h
match it up:  f = sin−1,  a = 45
so the expression is (sin−1)′ at x = 4/5.
No L'Hôpital needed — this is a derivative in disguise.
Step 2 — The derivative of arcsine
ddx sin−1(x) = 1√1 − x2
no minus sign here — that's arccosine. Arcsine's derivative is positive.
Memorize the trio: arcsin, arccos, arctan — and which one carries the minus.
Step 3 — Evaluate at x = 4/5
1√1 − (45)2 = 1√1 − 1625
= 1√925 = 135
1 − 16/25 = 9/25, and √(9/25) = 3/5.
Step 4 — Finish the division
13/5 = 1 · 53 = 53
Dividing by 3/5 is the same as multiplying by 5/3.
The answer
(C)  53
(A) 0 (B) 2541 (C) 53 (D) does not exist
(D) is the trap — sin−1 is differentiable at 4/5, so the limit exists. It's just the derivative.
