The question
If f(x) = √x2 − 4  and  g(x) = 3x − 2,
find the derivative of g(f(x)) at x = 6.
Two functions, nested together — that's a job for the chain rule.
Step 1 — Compose
g(f(x)) = 3 − 2
√x2 − 4
g(f(x)) = 3√x2 − 4 − 2
Substitute the blue f into the orange g's x-slot.
Step 2 — Chain rule
outer  3u − 2,  where u = √x2 − 4   inner
ddx[g(f(x))] = g′(f(x)) · f′(x)
outer:  ddu[3u − 2] = 3
inner:  ddx[√x2 − 4] = 12√x2 − 4 · 2x
the 2s cancel:  12√x2 − 4 · 2x = x√x2 − 4
multiply:  3 · x√x2 − 4 = 3x√x2 − 4
Step 3 — Simplify
ddx [g(f(x))] = 3x / √x2 − 4
Already a single fraction — nothing left to cancel.
Step 4 — Evaluate at x = 6
3 · 6√62 − 4 = 18√32
√32 = √16 · 2 = 4√2   ⇒   184√2
rationalize:  184√2 · √2√2 = 18√28 = 9√24
Multiply top and bottom by √2 to clear the radical from the denominator.
The answer
g′(f(6)) = 9√24 ≈ 3.18
